## 1. Digital footprint

Definition: A digital footprint is the trail of data or information a person leaves behind while using the internet and digital services.

Examples include social-media posts, comments, website visits, online purchases, uploaded photographs, search activity, and information collected by websites.

### Types of digital footprint

### A. Active digital footprint

Information a person deliberately shares or submits online.

Examples:

- Posting a photograph on Instagram.
    
- Writing a comment on YouTube.
    
- Sending an email or filling out an online form.
    
- Publishing a blog post.
    

### B. Passive digital footprint

Information collected or recorded through online activity, often without the person deliberately submitting each individual piece of data.

Examples:

- A website recording browsing activity.
    
- Cookies tracking website preferences.
    
- An application collecting usage statistics.
    
- A website recording an IP address in its logs.
    

### Active vs passive digital footprint

|Active footprint|Passive footprint|
|---|---|
|Deliberately shared or submitted|Collected or recorded during activity|
|Posting a status|Website recording visits|
|Uploading a résumé|Usage analytics|
|Writing a public review|Certain tracking cookies|

Important: Passive does not necessarily mean secret or illegal. Websites may disclose tracking in their privacy notices or ask for consent where required.

### Why does a digital footprint matter?

- Privacy: Personal information may become accessible to unintended audiences.
    
- Reputation: Old posts and comments may influence how others perceive a person.
    
- Security: Publicly available details can help attackers create targeted scams.
    
- Employment and education: Recruiters or institutions may review publicly available online content.
    
- Personalisation: Websites may use collected information to recommend content or advertisements.
    

### How to manage a digital footprint

1. Think before posting or sharing.
    
2. Review privacy settings regularly.
    
3. Avoid sharing passwords, OTPs, financial details, or unnecessary personal information.
    
4. Check app permissions and cookie settings.
    
5. Delete unwanted posts where possible and request removal of personal information when appropriate.
    
6. Use strong, unique passwords and multifactor authentication.
    

Exam trap: Deleting a post does not guarantee that it disappears everywhere. Others may have saved, copied, screenshotted, or archived it.

## 2. Netiquette

Definition: Netiquette means network etiquette — the rules and conventions for polite, respectful, and responsible behaviour while communicating online.

It applies to emails, discussion forums, online classes, chat rooms, gaming communities, and other internet-based interactions.

### Important rules of netiquette

- Be respectful and polite to others.
    
- Do not engage in cyberbullying, trolling, harassment, or personal attacks.
    
- Respect other people's privacy.
    
- Avoid spreading rumours, misinformation, or unverified content.
    
- Give credit to original creators; do not plagiarise.
    
- Avoid typing entire messages in CAPITAL LETTERS because they can be perceived as shouting.
    
- Keep messages relevant to the discussion.
    
- Do not spam or repeatedly send unwanted messages.
    
- Follow the rules of the platform or online community.
    
- Remember that there is a real person behind the screen.
    

### Examples

|Situation|Good netiquette|Poor netiquette|
|---|---|---|
|Online discussion|Respectfully disagree|Insult another participant|
|Sharing information|Verify before forwarding|Forward an unverified rumour|
|Using someone’s work|Credit the creator|Copy without attribution|
|Group chat|Send relevant messages|Flood the group with spam|
|Online disagreement|Discuss the issue calmly|Harass or threaten someone|

### Netiquette and cyberbullying

Cyberbullying is bullying or harassment carried out through digital devices or online platforms.

Examples include repeatedly sending threatening messages, publicly humiliating someone online, spreading harmful rumours, or sharing private images without permission.

Netiquette discourages such behaviour, but serious online harassment may also violate platform rules or applicable laws.

## 3. Communication etiquette

Definition: Communication etiquette refers to the accepted rules of clear, appropriate, professional, and respectful communication.

In digital contexts, it includes email writing, instant messaging, video meetings, online interviews, and professional communication.

### A. Email etiquette

1. Use a meaningful subject line.
    
2. Start with an appropriate greeting.
    
3. Keep the message clear, concise, and organised.
    
4. Use professional language and correct spelling.
    
5. Use CC to keep relevant recipients informed and BCC when recipients' addresses should be hidden from one another.
    
6. Verify recipients and attachments before sending.
    
7. Avoid sending confidential information to unauthorised recipients.
    
8. Use an appropriate closing and signature.
    

Example of a professional email structure:

Subject: Request for Project Status Update

Dear Team,

Could you please share the current status of the project when convenient?

Thank you for your support.

Regards, [Your Name]

### B. Instant messaging etiquette

- Keep messages concise and relevant.
    
- Avoid repeatedly messaging someone who has not responded.
    
- Respect working hours and availability.
    
- Do not share screenshots or private messages without appropriate permission.
    
- Use emojis and abbreviations according to the context.
    
- Avoid treating every message as an emergency.
    

### C. Video-conferencing etiquette

- Join on time.
    
- Check your microphone, camera, and internet connection.
    
- Mute your microphone when not speaking, especially in large meetings.
    
- Avoid interrupting speakers.
    
- Use the raise-hand feature when appropriate.
    
- Maintain a professional background and behaviour.
    
- Do not record meetings or share meeting links without authorisation.
    

### D. Professional online communication

Use a polite tone, provide relevant context, acknowledge messages appropriately, and distinguish facts from opinions. When disagreeing, address the issue rather than attacking the person.

Exam trap: Communication etiquette is the broader concept of communicating appropriately; netiquette specifically concerns conduct in online or networked environments. Their meanings overlap, but they are not identical.

## 4. Social-media etiquette

Definition: Social-media etiquette refers to the rules of responsible, respectful, and safe behaviour on platforms such as social networks, video-sharing sites, and online communities.

### Essential rules

1. Think before posting

Consider whether the content is accurate, appropriate, respectful, and safe to share. Posts can be copied or redistributed beyond the intended audience.

2. Respect privacy and consent

- Ask permission before posting identifiable photographs of others when appropriate.
    
- Do not disclose another person's phone number, address, private messages, or sensitive information without authorisation.
    
- Never share intimate or private images without consent.
    

3. Verify information before sharing

Check the source, date, context, and credibility of a claim. Avoid forwarding manipulated images, fake news, or misleading headlines.

4. Respect intellectual property

Credit original creators and follow copyright and licence conditions. Giving credit alone does not automatically grant permission to reuse copyrighted material.

5. Avoid online conflicts

Do not engage in trolling, harassment, hate speech, or abusive arguments. Report harmful content using the platform's reporting tools where appropriate.

6. Maintain account security

Use unique passwords, enable multifactor authentication, review login sessions, and be cautious about suspicious links and impersonation accounts.

7. Maintain a professional digital identity

Public posts and comments may affect personal and professional reputation. Use suitable privacy settings, but do not assume that private posts can never be exposed.

### Common social-media risks

|Risk|Meaning / example|Safer practice|
|---|---|---|
|Oversharing|Posting travel plans or personal details publicly|Limit unnecessary personal information|
|Fake news|Sharing a fabricated claim|Verify through reliable sources|
|Impersonation|Someone creates an account pretending to be you|Report the account and secure your profile|
|Phishing|A message tricks you into revealing credentials|Check links and never disclose OTPs|
|Cyberbullying|Repeated online harassment|Save evidence, block and report, and seek help|
|Privacy violation|Sharing someone's private information without permission|Obtain appropriate consent|

## 5. Important distinctions for MCQs

|Concept|Main focus|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Digital footprint|Information trail left online|A public comment or recorded browsing activity|
|Netiquette|Appropriate behaviour on the internet|Respectful participation in a forum|
|Communication etiquette|Appropriate communication style and conduct|A clear, polite professional email|
|Social-media etiquette|Responsible conduct on social platforms|Verifying a post before sharing it|

These concepts overlap in practice. For example, publishing a private photograph without permission can violate social-media etiquette, create privacy risks, and affect both people's digital footprints.
