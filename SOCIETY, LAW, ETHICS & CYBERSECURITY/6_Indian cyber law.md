## 1. Information Technology (IT) Act, 2000

Definition: The Information Technology Act, 2000 is an Indian law that provides legal recognition to electronic records and electronic signatures and establishes a legal framework for specified cyber-related offences and liabilities.

It came into force in 2000 and was substantially amended in 2008.

### Objectives of the IT Act

- Provide legal recognition to electronic records.
    
- Recognise electronic signatures under the statutory framework.
    
- Facilitate electronic commerce and electronic filing.
    
- Define certain cyber offences and associated penalties.
    
- Establish legal provisions relating to intermediary liability and specified cybersecurity matters.
    

### Important concepts

|Concept|Meaning|
|---|---|
|Electronic record|Information, data, or records in electronic form|
|Electronic signature|An electronic method recognised by law for authenticating an electronic record|
|Digital signature|A type of electronic signature using the statutory cryptographic framework|
|Intermediary|An entity that receives, stores, transmits, or provides services relating to electronic records on behalf of others, within the statutory definition|
|Cyber offence|An offence involving computer resources, electronic systems, or digital activity, as defined by applicable law|

Exam trap: Every digital signature is an electronic signature under the applicable framework, but not every electronic signature is necessarily a digital signature.

### Very important legal point: Section 66A

Section 66A is not a valid enforceable offence provision. The Supreme Court struck it down in Shreya Singhal v. Union of India on 24 March 2015. It concerned sending offensive messages through communication services. Do not memorise it as an active offence section.

### Section 43 vs Section 66

- Section 43: Covers specified unauthorised acts involving computer resources and provides for compensation under its framework.
    
- Section 66: Applies when an act referred to in Section 43 is done dishonestly or fraudulently, attracting criminal punishment.
    

Example: Unauthorised access to a computer system may raise Section 43 issues; if the relevant conduct meets Section 66's dishonest or fraudulent requirements, criminal liability may also arise.

## 3. Cyber offences: examples and section mapping

|Scenario|Relevant provision to remember|
|---|---|
|Unauthorised access or damage to a computer resource|Sections 43 and, where its requirements are met, 66|
|Stealing someone's password and using it fraudulently|Section 66C may apply|
|Pretending to be a bank representative online to cheat a victim|Section 66D may apply|
|Publishing a qualifying obscene electronic publication|Section 67 may apply|
|Capturing or sharing specified private-area images without consent in circumstances violating privacy|Section 66E may apply|
|Specified cyberterrorism conduct|Section 66F|
|Specified disclosure of confidential information by someone who obtained access under powers conferred by the Act|Section 72|

These are study examples, not automatic legal conclusions: the precise facts and statutory requirements determine which provision applies. Other laws may also apply to the same conduct.

Exam trap: An ordinary online disagreement or offensive post does not automatically constitute an offence under a particular IT Act section. Identify the exact legal elements instead of assuming every unpleasant online action is covered by the same provision.

## 4. Digital transactions

Definition: A digital transaction is a financial or commercial transaction initiated, processed, or recorded electronically rather than entirely through physical cash or paper-based processes.

Examples include UPI payments, internet banking, debit and credit card payments, electronic fund transfers, and transactions through digital wallets.

### Electronic signatures and digital transactions

Electronic records and electronic signatures help support electronic contracts, applications, and other transactions.

- Electronic record: Information stored or communicated electronically.
    
- Electronic signature: A legally recognised electronic method used to authenticate an electronic record.
    
- Digital signature: A specific cryptographic form of electronic signature.
    
- Authentication: Verifying the identity or authority of a person or system.
    
- Encryption: Protecting information by converting it into a form that cannot readily be understood without the appropriate key.
    

The IT Act provides the legal framework for electronic records and electronic signatures, subject to statutory exceptions and requirements.

### Digital payment safety

- Never share your UPI PIN, OTP, card PIN, CVV, or banking password with another person.
    
- Enter your UPI PIN only in the authorised payment app when you intend to authorise a payment; receiving money does not require entering your UPI PIN.
    
- Verify the recipient's name and payment amount before authorising a transaction.
    
- Do not install remote-access applications at the request of an unverified caller.
    
- Use official banking applications and websites.
    
- Enable transaction alerts and report unauthorised transactions to your bank promptly.
    

These precautions align with guidance from the RBI and NPCI.
