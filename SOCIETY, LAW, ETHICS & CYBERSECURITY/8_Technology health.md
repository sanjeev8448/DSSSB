## Topic 8: Technology and Health — DSSSB TGT Computer Science

Technology and health covers the effects of using computers and digital devices on physical health, mental well-being, and daily behaviour.

For your exam, focus on three areas:

1. Ergonomics
    
2. Screen-related health issues
    
3. Responsible technology use
    

## 1. Ergonomics

Definition: Ergonomics is the science of designing workplaces, equipment, and tasks to fit human capabilities and limitations, improving comfort, safety, and efficiency.

In computer use, ergonomics helps reduce physical strain caused by prolonged or incorrect use of computers, keyboards, mice, and screens.

1. Monitor position

- Keep the screen approximately an arm's length away, adjusting for comfort and screen size.
    
- Position the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level in a typical seated setup.
    
- Avoid glare and excessive brightness.

2. Sitting posture

- Keep your back supported.
    
- Relax your shoulders.
    
- Keep your feet supported on the floor or a footrest.
    
- Adjust the chair so your knees and hips are comfortably positioned.
    

3. Keyboard and mouse

- Keep them close enough to avoid reaching.
    
- Keep wrists relatively straight.
    
- Use a light touch rather than excessive force.
    
- Keep elbows comfortably near your body.
    

### Benefits of ergonomics

- Reduces muscle strain and discomfort.
    
- Helps prevent repetitive strain injuries.
    
- Improves comfort and work efficiency.
    
- Encourages better posture.
    
- Can reduce neck, shoulder, wrist, and back discomfort.
    

### Common ergonomic problems

|Problem|Possible cause|
|---|---|
|Neck pain|Screen positioned too high or low, or prolonged static posture|
|Back pain|Poor seating support or prolonged sitting|
|Wrist discomfort|Awkward wrist position or repetitive movements|
|Shoulder strain|Reaching too far for the keyboard or mouse|
|Eye discomfort|Glare, poor viewing conditions, or prolonged near work|

Exam point: Ergonomics is not only about posture. It also includes the design of equipment, the work environment, and how tasks are organised.

## 2. Screen-related health issues

Prolonged or poorly managed screen use can contribute to eye discomfort, musculoskeletal strain, sleep disruption, and reduced physical activity.

### A. Digital eye strain

Definition: Digital eye strain is a group of eye-related symptoms associated with prolonged use of computers, smartphones, tablets, and other digital screens.

Common symptoms include:

- Dry or irritated eyes.
    
- Blurred vision.
    
- Headaches.
    
- Difficulty refocusing between near and distant objects.
    
- Eye fatigue.
    

Prevention:

- Follow the 20-20-20 rule: approximately every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds.
    
- Blink regularly.
    
- Reduce glare and adjust screen brightness for the surroundings.
    
- Position the screen at a comfortable distance.
    
- Take regular breaks from close-up work.
    

### B. Musculoskeletal problems

Long periods in a fixed position, poor posture, or repetitive movements may contribute to neck, back, shoulder, and wrist discomfort.

Prevention: Adjust the workstation, vary your posture, take movement breaks, and avoid prolonged repetitive activity without rest.

### C. Sleep disruption

Using screens late at night may delay sleep or interfere with sleep routines because of stimulating content, notifications, and exposure to light.

Prevention:

- Maintain a consistent sleep schedule.
    
- Reduce stimulating screen activities before bedtime.
    
- Silence unnecessary notifications at night.
    
- Keep devices away from the bed when they interfere with sleep.
    

### D. Sedentary behaviour

Extended screen time can mean less physical movement, especially when sitting continuously for hours.

Possible consequences of prolonged sedentary behaviour include reduced physical fitness and increased long-term health risks.

Prevention: Stand up, stretch, walk briefly, and include regular physical activity in your routine.

### E. Mental well-being

Unmanaged technology use may contribute to stress, distraction, information overload, social comparison, and difficulty concentrating.

Helpful practices include setting limits, disabling unnecessary notifications, maintaining offline activities, and seeking support when technology use begins to interfere with everyday life.

### Quick comparison

|Issue|Common symptoms or effects|Prevention|
|---|---|---|
|Digital eye strain|Dry eyes, fatigue, blurred vision|Breaks, blinking, reduced glare|
|Musculoskeletal strain|Neck, back, shoulder, wrist discomfort|Ergonomic setup and movement|
|Sleep disruption|Difficulty falling asleep or poor sleep routine|Reduce late-night stimulation|
|Sedentary behaviour|Extended inactivity|Regular movement and exercise|
|Digital overload|Distraction, stress, reduced concentration|Manage notifications and screen habits|

Exam trap: Digital eye strain is not the same as permanent eye damage. Screen-related symptoms can have several causes; persistent or severe symptoms warrant professional assessment.

## 3. Responsible technology use

Definition: Responsible technology use means using digital devices, applications, and online services safely, ethically, respectfully, and in a balanced way.

It includes protecting personal information, respecting others, managing screen time, checking information before sharing it, and following applicable rules.

### Key principles

1. Digital well-being

- Balance screen activities with sleep, exercise, and offline activities.
    
- Take breaks during long periods of device use.
    
- Avoid letting notifications continually interrupt work or study.
    

2. Privacy and security

- Use strong, unique passwords and multifactor authentication.
    
- Review app permissions.
    
- Avoid sharing sensitive information publicly.
    
- Keep devices and software updated.
    

3. Ethical behaviour

- Respect other people's privacy and intellectual property.
    
- Do not engage in cyberbullying, trolling, or online harassment.
    
- Give credit to creators where required.
    
- Do not spread unverified information.
    

4. Digital literacy

- Evaluate the credibility of websites and online claims.
    
- Recognise phishing attempts and misinformation.
    
- Understand that online content may be misleading or manipulated.
    
- Check the source and date before relying on information.
    

5. Digital balance

- Set realistic limits for recreational screen use.
    
- Keep study and work sessions focused.
    
- Make time for physical activity and face-to-face interaction.
    
- Use technology to support productivity rather than allowing it to become a constant distraction.
    

### Healthy vs irresponsible technology habits

|Responsible habit|Irresponsible habit|
|---|---|
|Taking regular movement breaks|Sitting for hours without moving|
|Using unique passwords|Reusing one password everywhere|
|Verifying information before sharing|Forwarding rumours immediately|
|Respecting online privacy|Posting others' private information without permission|
|Managing notifications|Constantly checking every notification|
|Maintaining a balanced sleep routine|Allowing late-night screen use to regularly disrupt sleep|

### Example

A student preparing for an examination uses a laptop for online classes, takes short breaks, keeps notifications muted during study sessions, verifies information from reliable sources, and avoids using someone else's work without attribution. This is an example of responsible technology use.
