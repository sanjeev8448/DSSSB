## Topic 7: E-waste — DSSSB TGT Computer Science

E-waste is an important environmental and technology topic. For the exam, focus on its sources, hazardous substances, safe disposal, management methods, and environmental effects.

## 1. What is e-waste?

Definition: Electronic waste (e-waste) refers to discarded electrical and electronic equipment, including components and parts that have reached the end of their useful life or are no longer wanted.

Examples include old computers, laptops, mobile phones, printers, televisions, circuit boards, chargers, and electronic batteries.

### Why is e-waste increasing?

- Rapid technological development and frequent device replacement.
    
- Short product lifecycles and changing consumer preferences.
    
- Increased use of computers, smartphones, and connected devices.
    
- Expensive or difficult repairs.
    
- Inadequate collection and recycling systems.
    

Exam point: E-waste is not limited to computers. It includes many types of discarded electrical and electronic equipment.

## 3. Hazards of e-waste

Electronic devices may contain hazardous substances. If they are dismantled, burned, or processed improperly, these substances can harm people and the environment.

|Substance / material|Possible source|Potential hazard|
|---|---|---|
|Lead|Some solder, older electronic components, certain displays|Nervous-system and developmental harm|
|Mercury|Certain lamps and older electronic components|Nervous-system and kidney damage|
|Cadmium|Some batteries and electronic components|Kidney damage and other toxic effects|
|Hexavalent chromium compounds|Certain coatings and industrial materials|Some compounds are carcinogenic|
|Brominated flame retardants|Certain plastics and electronic enclosures|Some can pose health and environmental risks|
|Lithium-ion batteries|Phones, laptops, tablets|Fire and thermal runaway if damaged or mishandled|

### Routes of exposure

- Air: Burning cables and electronic waste can release hazardous fumes and particles.
    
- Soil: Improper dumping can contaminate soil.
    
- Water: Leaching from waste can contaminate groundwater and surface water.
    
- Direct contact: Informal dismantling may expose workers to hazardous materials.
    

Exam trap: Not every electronic component is equally hazardous. The risk depends on the material, device, exposure route, and how the waste is handled.

## 4. Disposal of e-waste

Safe disposal means ensuring discarded electronics are reused, refurbished, recycled, or treated through appropriate authorised channels rather than dumped or burned with ordinary waste.

### Recommended disposal methods

1. Reuse

Continue using a functional device when it remains suitable for its purpose.

2. Repair and refurbishment

Repair or restore equipment to extend its useful life.

3. Recycling

Recover useful materials such as copper, aluminium, and selected precious metals through appropriate recycling processes.

4. Safe treatment and recovery

Separate hazardous components and handle them using suitable equipment and authorised facilities.

### What should you do with an old laptop or phone?

1. Check whether it can be repaired, reused, or refurbished.
    
2. Back up important data.
    
3. Sign out of accounts and securely erase personal data where possible.
    
4. Remove SIM and memory cards.
    
5. Use an authorised e-waste collection point, producer take-back programme, or registered recycler.
    
6. Do not burn cables, break batteries, or dismantle hazardous components at home.
    

Important: Damaged or swollen lithium-ion batteries should not be placed in ordinary rubbish or handled in a way that can puncture or crush them. Follow local battery collection and safety guidance.

## 5. E-waste management

Definition: E-waste management is the systematic collection, segregation, storage, transportation, reuse, recycling, and safe treatment of discarded electrical and electronic equipment.

### Main stages of e-waste management

#### 1

### Collection

Collect discarded electronic equipment separately.

#### 2

### Segregation

Separate reusable devices, recyclable parts, and hazardous components.

#### 3

### Reuse and refurbishment

Extend the life of equipment where practical.

#### 4

### Authorised recycling

Recover useful materials using appropriate processes.

#### 5

### Safe treatment and disposal

Manage remaining hazardous waste through suitable facilities.

### Important management strategies

- Reduce: Avoid unnecessary purchases and replace devices only when needed.
    
- Reuse: Pass working devices to another user or organisation.
    
- Repair: Fix devices rather than discarding them immediately.
    
- Recycle: Recover valuable materials and reduce demand for new raw materials.
    
- Responsible producer practices: Design longer-lasting, repairable products and provide appropriate take-back or recycling arrangements.
    
- Public awareness: Educate people about safe collection, disposal, and data protection.
    

### E-waste management in India

For exam purposes, remember these basic concepts:

- The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 provides the broader legal framework for environmental protection.
    
- The E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 introduced an important framework for managing e-waste, including extended producer responsibility (EPR).
    
- Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) means producers have specified responsibilities for managing the end-of-life stage of products they place on the market, according to the applicable rules.
    
- The Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) plays an important role in implementing and administering relevant e-waste regulatory processes.
    

Exam trap: EPR is not simply a consumer responsibility. It places defined responsibilities on producers under the applicable regulatory framework.

## 6. Environmental effects of e-waste

Improper handling and disposal of e-waste can affect air, water, soil, ecosystems, and human health.

|Environmental effect|How it occurs|Possible consequence|
|---|---|---|
|Air pollution|Burning wires, plastics, and circuit boards|Toxic fumes and particulate emissions|
|Soil contamination|Dumping or leaching of hazardous materials|Reduced soil quality and contamination|
|Water pollution|Hazardous substances entering groundwater or rivers|Harm to aquatic life and possible human exposure|
|Ecosystem damage|Contaminants entering food chains|Toxic effects on plants and animals|
|Resource depletion|Discarding recoverable materials|Greater demand for mining and raw materials|
|Greenhouse-gas emissions|Manufacturing replacement devices and inefficient waste treatment|Increased lifecycle emissions|

### Why recycling helps the environment

- Recovers metals and other useful materials.
    
- Reduces the need for some new mining.
    
- Helps prevent hazardous materials from being released through improper disposal.
    
- Can reduce waste sent to landfills.
    
- Supports a circular economy by keeping resources in use for longer.
    

However, recycling must be performed safely. Informal recycling involving open burning, acid leaching, or unsafe dismantling can cause serious pollution and occupational exposure.
