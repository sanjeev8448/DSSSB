## 1. Augmented Reality (AR)

Definition: Augmented Reality is a technology that overlays digital content—such as images, text, animations, or 3D objects—onto the user's view of the real-world environment.

In simple words, AR adds digital elements to the real world.

Example: Virtual furniture placement

A smartphone app displays a virtual sofa on the camera view of your actual living room, helping you see how it might look before buying it.

### Applications of AR

- Education: Displays 3D models of the human body, planets, or machines.
    
- Healthcare: Supports visualization of anatomical information.
    
- Retail: Lets customers preview furniture or accessories.
    
- Navigation: Displays directions over a camera view.
    
- Maintenance: Shows digital instructions over physical equipment.
    
- Gaming: Places virtual objects or characters into real-world surroundings.
    

Exam keyword: Digital content overlaid on the real world.

## 2. Virtual Reality (VR)

Definition: Virtual Reality is a technology that creates a computer-generated, immersive environment in which users can look around and interact with a simulated world.

In simple words, VR replaces the user's ordinary view with a virtual environment.

![Man entering virtual reality](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/-3j2YypXWkgLi6MwOxN7FiT_zVju6V5tvtSu-AW5GUOYHmD_tXI9sc6tkQ3vXNnGuyCaEhpcT_F5IMVUVknzbN7Laaqm4MOvMHVavDpD-DwRpdPv8Ska-iFe9KBDnYL0jxMgygMpMkj0nuAMMzm4xyDOPbuYLvqKHM26PqR6uDa6uGNhDgRMt3QYMOuxbWJT?purpose=inline)

Example: Flight simulator

A trainee wears a VR headset and experiences a simulated cockpit to practise flying in a virtual environment without operating a real aircraft.

### Applications of VR

- Education and training: Simulated laboratory experiments and practical training.
    
- Healthcare: Surgical training and selected therapeutic applications.
    
- Gaming and entertainment: Immersive virtual worlds.
    
- Aviation: Flight simulation for pilots.
    
- Architecture: Exploring a virtual building before construction.
    
- Industrial training: Practising procedures in simulated environments.
    

Exam keyword: Immersive, computer-generated environment.

## 3. AR vs VR — Key differences

|Basis|Augmented Reality (AR)|Virtual Reality (VR)|
|---|---|---|
|Full form|Augmented Reality|Virtual Reality|
|Environment|Real world enhanced with digital elements|Computer-generated virtual environment|
|User's view|Real surroundings remain visible, with digital overlays|Ordinary surroundings are generally replaced by the virtual view|
|Immersion|Usually partial|Usually more immersive|
|Common devices|Smartphones, tablets, AR glasses|VR headsets and motion controllers|
|Interaction|Interacts with physical surroundings and digital objects|Interacts with objects in the simulated environment|
|Example|Placing virtual furniture in a real room|Exploring a completely virtual room|

### Easy memory trick

- AR = Adds digital objects to reality.
    
- VR = Virtual world replaces the ordinary view.
    

## 4. Related term: Mixed Reality (MR)

Mixed Reality (MR) combines physical and digital environments in ways that can allow digital objects to interact with or respond to the real environment.

Example: A virtual 3D model is anchored to a real table and remains positioned there as the user moves around it.

For basic exam preparation, remember:

- AR: Digital overlay on the real world.
    
- VR: Immersion in a virtual world.
    
- MR: Physical and digital elements can interact.
